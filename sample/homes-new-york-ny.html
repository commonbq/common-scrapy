Why Live in New York

New York City's iconic landmarks create an unmatched urban experience, from the 843-acre Central Park to the neon-lit excitement of Times Square. The elevated High Line park provides unique Manhattan views, while the Statue of Liberty stands as a symbol of freedom in New York Harbor. Cultural institutions include the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the American Museum of Natural History. The dining scene features world-renowned establishments like Restaurant Daniel, Le Bernardin, and Eleven Madison Park, each delivering exceptional culinary experiences.The real estate landscape varies significantly across the five boroughs. Manhattan condos command the highest prices, with two-bedroom units reaching a median price of $1,190,000. One-bedroom condos have a median price of $680,000, while studio condos start at a median of $320,000. Townhouses throughout the city have a median price of $969,000. Single-family homes show a median price of $749,000.Property taxes in New York County stand at 0.9%, lower than nearby Jersey City at 1.85% and Newark at 2.02%. The state income tax rate is 5.50% for a yearly salary of $42,741, with a combined sales tax rate of 8.53%. The extensive public transportation system eliminates the need for a car. The 24/7 subway network connects all boroughs, complemented by buses, taxis, and the Citi Bike program. Educational institutions include Columbia University, New York University, and CUNY City College.
